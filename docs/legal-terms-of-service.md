# Terms of Service — draft content for /terms-of-service/

**READ THIS FIRST.** Same caution as the privacy policy: this is a drafted
starting point, not legal advice, and it needs a Singapore-qualified lawyer
to review before it goes live.

One structural decision is worth making deliberately before you publish.
There are two different documents people call "terms of service":

1. **Website terms** — the rules for using this website: what the content
   is, what you may do with it, and the limits of our responsibility for
   what is published here.
2. **Terms of engagement** — the commercial terms under which client work is
   done: scope, fees, payment, intellectual property in deliverables,
   confidentiality, termination.

The draft below is the **first kind**. That is the right choice for a
public page, because your client work is governed by the signed contract for
each engagement, and a public page that appears to set commercial terms can
create confusion about which document actually governs. There is a short
clause at the end saying exactly that.

If you would rather publish commercial terms as well, that is a different
document and it should be drafted with your lawyer from your actual contract,
not adapted from a website page.

One further caution, on governing law. You asked for both Singapore and
Malaysia. A contract normally has a single governing law, so the draft names
Singapore as governing while expressly preserving Malaysian mandatory
protections and leaving both courts available. That is how a two-country
intent is usually honoured without creating a conflict. The clause carries a
CONFIRM marker explaining it.
